<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.1</div>

## Delegation

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Decide what to do yourself, and what to hand AI
- Choose the right mode for each task
- Write a delegation plan for your project

Note: Lesson title card. The first D, and the strategic front-end. Bad delegation makes every later step wasteful.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.1 · What it is</div>

# It starts with a decision

Before AI does anything, you decide: should it do this task, and which parts?

- Not everything belongs to AI, and not every part of a task
- This one choice shapes the quality of everything that follows

**Hand over the drafts. Keep the decisions that last.**

Note: Anthropic's definition in plain words, delegation is the decision at the start, whether AI should do a task and which parts. Why: bad delegation wastes effort or risks a bad outcome, it is strategic, not just offloading. The rule to remember, hand over drafts and reversible work, keep the judgement-heavy, high-stakes calls. This sets up the next slide, the three decisions.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.1 · How to delegate</div>

# Three decisions before you prompt

- **Know the goal** break the task into parts, and spot the steps that need real judgement
- **Know the tool** AI is strong at drafts and options, weak at private facts and final calls
- **Split the work** hand AI the drafts, keep the judgement and the final call yourself

Pick the mode too, on purpose: automation, augmentation, or agency.

Example: AI drafts the gym page copy, you choose the offer and which classes to feature.

Note: The three delegation sub-skills in one slide, know the goal (Problem/Goal Awareness), know the tool (Platform Awareness), split the work (Task Delegation), plus picking the mode from Module 3. Keep it tight, this is the how of delegation. The gym example shows the split.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.1 · Your turn</div>

# Plan your project

<p class="try"><b>Your turn</b> Break your project into 4 to 6 tasks. For each, write who does it: AI, you, or both. That is your delegation plan.</p>

<span class="demo">Demo</span>

Note: This is Anthropic's project-planning exercise. Do it live on the gym landing page: the offer and which classes to feature (you), the headline and copy drafts (AI), layout (AI draft, you approve), photos (AI suggests, you use real ones), final check on prices and class times (you). Save this plan, every later lesson builds on it.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.1 · Recap</div>

# Quick recap

- Delegation is deciding whether, when, and how to use AI
- Know your goal, know the tools, then split the work
- Hand over drafts, keep the decisions that last

Note: Ten second reminder. Next, once you have decided AI does a task, you have to describe it clearly.

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.2</div>

## Description

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Describe what you want, the how, and the behaviour
- Give AI enough context to get it right
- Write a clear first prompt for your project

Note: Lesson title card. The second D. AI can only act on what you make explicit, so description is your input-quality lever.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.2 · What it is</div>

# Description: communicating clearly

Effectively describing your goal so AI produces useful work.

- AI is literal, it acts on what you say, not what you meant
- Most poor results come from thin descriptions, not weak AI
- The clearer you are, the better the result, every time

Note: Anthropic's definition. The core reframe, when the output is bad, the fix is usually a better description, not a better tool. Do not pre-list the parts here, the next two slides introduce them, product (the output) and process (how you work together), plus behaviour as a light bullet for when AI acts for others.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.2 · Sub-skill 1</div>

# Product Description

Describe the output you want.

- What is it, who is it for, what format, how long?
- Tone, style, and any must-haves or must-avoids

Instead of "make me a landing page":

<div class="promptcard"><span class="lbl">Better</span>

A one-page landing page for my small neighbourhood gym. Energetic and welcoming. One headline, three benefits, our class types, the monthly price, and a Start free trial button.

</div>

Note: The first sub-competency, describing the what, the artifact. This is the most familiar kind of prompting. Notice how much the second version gives, audience, format, length, tone, priority.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.2 · Sub-skill 2</div>

# Process Description

Describe how you want to work together.

- Ask for steps, or one part at a time
- Tell it to ask you questions before it starts
- Say when to pause, expand, or slow down

Example: "Before you write, ask me what makes my gym different from the big chains."

Note: The second sub-competency, describing the how, the working relationship. This is what makes the collaboration iterative instead of one-shot. It feeds directly into the loop lesson coming up.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.2 · The test</div>

# The zero-guesses test

Read your prompt back, and count the guesses you left AI.

- Every guess is a chance to get it wrong
- Add the missing context until there are none

<p class="try"><b>Your turn</b> Write the first real prompt for your project. Use all three, the product, the process, and the behaviour if it needs one.</p>

Note: A simple self-check students can reuse forever. Zero guesses equals a good brief. Demo the test live on the landing page prompt, find a guess, close it. They write their own prompt now.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.2 · Recap</div>

# Quick recap

- Describe the product, the process, and the behaviour
- AI is literal, state the context you have in your head
- Count the guesses, then close them

Note: Ten second reminder. Next, six techniques that make any description sharper.

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.3</div>

## Six prompting techniques

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Use six reliable techniques to sharpen a prompt
- Spot why a weak prompt failed, and fix it
- Improve a bad prompt live

Note: Lesson title card. The practical toolkit for Description. Builds on the basics from Module 1, this goes deeper.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.3 · The toolkit</div>

# Six techniques that work

| Technique | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Give context | Say what, why, and the background |
| Show examples | Demonstrate the style or format you want |
| Set constraints | State format, length, and requirements |
| Break into steps | Split a complex task into parts |
| Ask it to think first | Let it reason before it answers |
| Give it a role | Set the persona or tone |

Note: Anthropic's six effective prompting techniques. Do not teach them as a checklist to run every time, teach them as fixes to reach for when a prompt is not working. Names are confirmed, keep the glosses plain.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.3 · In action</div>

# Weak to strong

<div class="promptcard"><span class="lbl">Weak</span>

Write about my gym.

</div>

<div class="promptcard"><span class="lbl">Strong</span>

You are a fitness copywriter. Turn these three gym features into three short benefit lines, each starting with a verb. Ask me who the gym is for if it is unclear.

</div>

Techniques used: a role, constraints, and think-first.

Note: This is the six techniques in action, not just another before and after. The strong version stacks a role ("fitness copywriter"), constraints ("three lines, start with a verb"), and think-first ("ask me who the gym is for"). Read the weak one aloud, then the strong one, and point to each technique. This differentiates it from the Product Description card in Lesson 4.2, which was about what to include, this is about how to phrase.

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.3 · Recap</div>

# Quick recap

- Six techniques: context, examples, constraints, steps, think-first, role
- Reach for the one that fixes what went wrong
- A quick makeover turns a weak prompt strong

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.4</div>

## Discernment

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Spot when a confident answer is actually wrong
- Run three quick checks before you trust it

Note: Lesson title card. The third D, and the short one, because it lives mostly in the loop that follows. Two objectives only, each maps to one teaching slide, fluent-is-not-correct and the three checks. No restated bullets that all mean judge the answer.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.4 · The core idea</div>

# Fluent is not the same as correct

AI writes with confidence, whether it is right or wrong.

- Do not trust an answer just because it sounds polished
- Check it against reality, a source, your own knowledge
- The better AI gets, the more careful you must be

Example: AI writes "IronFit Gym, open 24/7, heated pool, 50+ weekly classes." You have no pool and run 12 classes. Every word confident, every word needs checking.

Note: The single most important point in the module, corroborated by every source. Confident, fluent output can be wrong. And as models improve, wrong answers get more convincing, so discernment is a growing skill, not a crutch you outgrow. The IronFit example, reads beautifully but invents a pool and inflates the class count, this is the hook to open the lesson.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.4 · Three checks</div>

# Three quick checks

- **The output** is it accurate and complete?
- **The reasoning** do the steps actually hold up?
- **The assumptions** what did it quietly take for granted?

<p class="try"><b>Your turn</b> Judge your project's draft. Note one thing to keep, and one thing to fix.</p>

Note: A simple, repeatable checklist, each check catches a different kind of mistake, mapped to the gym page. Output, the invented pool. Reasoning, a contradiction on the page, it says "open every day" at the top but "closed Sundays" lower down, the claims do not hold together, anyone can spot it. Assumptions, the free parking it assumed, the sneaky one people skip. Memorable universal examples, one per check, to say aloud so it clicks: output, "the Great Wall is visible from space with the naked eye" sounds like trivia but is false; reasoning, it says "Friday the 5th" but the 5th is a Saturday, the details do not line up; assumptions, "plan my Saturday picnic" and AI quietly assumes clear weather and a way to get to the park. They apply the three checks to their own draft now.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.4 · Recap</div>

# Quick recap

- Fluent is not correct, verify against reality
- Check the output, the reasoning, and the assumptions
- Keep it, refine it, or set it aside

Note: Ten second reminder. Next, Description and Discernment are really one loop, the engine of good AI work.

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.5</div>

## The Describe–Discern loop

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Run the loop with AI: ask, check, fix, then repeat
- Turn a rough first answer into a strong one over a few rounds

Note: Lesson title card. The engine of the whole framework, its own lesson because it is the centrepiece. Objectives kept in plain words, ask-check-fix-repeat is the loop (the engine slide names the real terms describe, discern, refine, integrate); the second is the habit (first answer is a draft). No jargon on the outcome slide.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.5 · The engine</div>

# Describe, discern, refine, repeat

**Describe  →  Discern  →  Refine  →  Integrate**

- **Describe** tell AI clearly what you want
- **Discern** judge what came back
- **Refine** say exactly what to change, and go again
- **Integrate** make it yours: add what only you know, and keep the best

Note: Anthropic's four-beat loop. Refine is asking AI to change something. Integrate is different, it is where you take over, blend AI's output with your own knowledge and judgement, and own the final result, you are not asking AI here, you are finishing it yourself. Do not expect perfection from one instruction, polishing through dialogue is the shortcut.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.5 · The mirror</div>

# Every describe has a discern

| You describe | You discern |
| --- | --- |
| The output you want | Is the output any good? |
| How you work together | Is the back-and-forth working? |

Note: The pedagogical spine, Description and Discernment mirror each other, that is why they are taught as one paired system. Kept to the two layers everyone uses, the output and the working style, in plain words. Dropped the third, niche "Performance" row (behaviour of a bot in agency mode), it confuses a general audience and we already folded Performance into a light bullet in Lesson 4.2.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.5 · The habit</div>

# The first answer is a draft

- Great results come a few rounds in, not on the first reply
- Each round sharpens both your request and the result

<div class="pullquote">"I don't fully know what I want until I see what I don't. The loop is how I find out."</div>

<p class="try"><b>Your turn</b> Run three rounds of the loop on your project. Watch it get better each time.</p>

<span class="demo">Demo</span>

Note: Frees people from expecting a perfect first answer. Demo three turns live on the landing page, a weak first draft, one sharp correction, a much better second, then a final polish. Reflection, what did each round fix?

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.5 · Recap</div>

# Quick recap

- Describe, discern, refine, integrate, then repeat
- Every describe has a matching discern
- The first answer is a draft, the loop makes it good

Note: Ten second reminder. Next, the final D, using AI responsibly and owning what you ship.

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.6</div>

## Diligence

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Use AI responsibly, and watch for bias
- Be honest about AI's role in your work
- Verify and own the final result

Note: Lesson title card. The fourth D, the ethical wrapper. You, not the AI, hold final accountability.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.6 · What it is</div>

# Diligence: taking responsibility

Taking responsibility for what you do with AI, and how.

- AI assisted does not mean AI accountable, your name is on the result
- Three habits make this real

Note: Anthropic's definition. The core line, you hold ultimate accountability for anything you act on, share, or publish. The three sub-competencies come next, creation, transparency, deployment.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.6 · Sub-skill 1</div>

# Creation Diligence

Build thoughtfully, from the start.

- Watch for bias and unfair or harmful output
- Pick a tool you are allowed to use for this work
- Protect private data, do not paste what is not yours

Example: building the gym page, do not paste real member data into AI, and do not ship copied or unsafe code.

Note: The first sub-competency, ethics during the work itself, bias, honesty, data protection, and tool choice. Dev angle, since the page is a build, the risks while making it are leaking private member data, shipping copied or insecure code, using a tool you are not allowed to, or inventing fake reviews. Sticky line for developers: "AI code compiles confidently and still breaks."

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.6 · Sub-skill 2</div>

# Transparency Diligence

Be honest about AI's role.

- Tell people when AI played a real part, do not pass it off as fully your own
- A simple line is enough: "drafted with AI, reviewed by me"

Note: The second sub-competency. Disclosure builds trust, and the level scales with stakes, a personal note needs none, a client deliverable or public report does. Transparency also protects you.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.6 · Sub-skill 3</div>

# Deployment Diligence

Check before it goes out.

- Fact-check claims, numbers, and dates against a reliable source
- Test that it actually works

Example: run the page yourself, test the sign-up button works, and check the price and class times are right.

Note: The third sub-competency, the final gate before release. Fact-check, test, validate. Dev angle, do not just read AI's code, run it, click the sign-up button, load it on a phone. Wrong class times or a broken button are deployment-diligence misses. This is where hallucinations get caught before they reach anyone.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.6 · The test</div>

# Would I put my name on this?

If the answer is no, it is not ready.

- Verify one fact you are relying on
- Name one group it could affect
- Decide honestly if you would sign it

<p class="try"><b>Your turn</b> On your project: is it clean, did you say AI helped, and does it actually work? If you would put your name on it, it is done.</p>

Note: The one-line test to remember, backed by the three habits applied to their own project, clean (Creation), disclosed (Transparency), works (Deployment). This is the apply-to-your-project step, same as every other lesson. Reflection, what would you change before you shipped it?

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 4.6 · Recap</div>

# Quick recap

- Create thoughtfully, watch for bias, protect data
- Be transparent about AI's role
- Verify, test, and own it, would you sign it?

Note: Ten second reminder. Next, put all four together and finish your project.
