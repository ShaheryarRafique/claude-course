<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1.1 · The big idea</div>

# A search box, or an assistant?

Most people use Claude like a search box. It works more like an assistant who does the task with you.

| A search box | Claude, an assistant |
| --- | --- |
| Answers one query, then stops | Works through a whole task with you |
| Starts fresh every time | Remembers your context and your files |
| Gives generic results | Uses your situation and your documents |
| Only returns links | Writes, builds, analyses, and takes direction |
| You take what you get | You steer, correct, and improve it |

Note: The core reframe from Claude 101, search box versus thinking partner, as a clean table. Claude is built by Anthropic to be helpful, honest, and harmless, and it is steerable, it takes direction on tone and style. Read the table one row at a time, left to right.

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1.2 · How to talk to it</div>

# Talk like a coworker

- Every message you send Claude is called a **prompt**
- **Type it, speak it, or dictate it** with the microphone, whatever is easier
- Speak naturally, there is no special syntax
- Start simple, then add detail as you go

A prompt can be a quick question or a full request. "How do I hang a picture frame?" and "Plan my revision for the next seven days" both work.

Note: Claude 101's framing: talk to Claude like a coworker or friend. Introduce the word "prompt", it is just your message. Show the three ways to enter one: type it, tap the microphone to speak, or dictate on mobile. Verify voice input availability before recording. This lowers the fear.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1.2 · The technique</div>

# Write a good prompt

A good prompt usually has three parts:

- **Set the stage** who you are, your goal, the context
- **Define the task** the exact action: write, analyse, plan, build
- **Specify the rules** length, tone, format, an example to copy

"I need a birthday gift for my sister who loves painting. Suggest five options I can buy locally, around fifty dollars, with a reason for each."

Note: Claude 101's prompt framework, set the stage, define the task, specify the rules. This is the technique they reuse for every prompt. Build this gift prompt on screen, pointing at the three parts. We run it on the next slide.

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1.3 · When the answer is off</div>

# Common complaints, and the fix

| The complaint | Why it happens | The fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Too generic | You left out your context | Add your role, audience, and limits |
| Wrong format | You said what, not how | Show an example, or name the structure |
| Confident but wrong | It guessed to fill a gap | Ask for sources, verify what matters |
| Wrong tone | It defaulted to neutral | Name the tone, or paste a sample |

Note: Claude 101's troubleshooting table, done as a real table: the complaint, why it happens, and the fix. Read one row at a time. The point to land: every fix is just a better prompt.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1.3 · See it in action</div>

# From weak to strong

Every fix is the same move: use the technique, stage, task, and rules.

| Instead of | Try |
| --- | --- |
| "Write about the delay" | "Email our client that the launch slips two weeks, apologetic, under 120 words, with a new date" |
| "Summarise this report" | "Summarise this report in five bullets, a bold header for each section" |
| "Give me some ideas" | "As a marketer, give me five campaign ideas for students, each in one line" |
| "Make it sound better" | "Rewrite this warm and confident, matching the sample I paste" |

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1.4 · Your model</div>

# Pick the right model

| When you need | Pick |
| --- | --- |
| Speed on a quick, simple task | Haiku |
| The everyday default, for most work | Sonnet |
| Deep reasoning on a hard problem | Opus |
| A large, long, multi-step project | Fable |

Free gives you Haiku and Sonnet. Opus and Fable are on the paid plans. Start on the everyday model, and move up only when the task needs it.

Note: Verified 2026-08-17, current lineup Opus 5, Sonnet 5, Haiku 4.5, Fable 5. Capability ladder is Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, Fable, with Fable built for long, autonomous work. Re-verify version numbers before recording, they change.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1.4 · Effort</div>

# Set the effort

Effort is how hard the model thinks before it answers.

- **Low** and **Medium** quick, simple tasks
- **High** the default, right for most work
- **Extra high** and **Max** the hardest reasoning, slower but deeper

High is already on by default, so turn it up only for genuinely hard problems. The top levels are not on every model.

Note: Verified 2026-08-17. Five levels: Low, Medium, High, Extra high (xhigh), Max. Default is High. Extra high and Max are not on Haiku. Effort and thinking are separate settings, this replaced the old "extended thinking" toggle.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1.4 · The toolbox</div>

# Turn on the right tool

Turn on what the task needs:

- **Web search** current facts, with links you can check
- **Research** a deep, cited report pulled from many sources over a few minutes

Research is on the paid plans and needs web search on. For hard thinking with no web, just raise the effort.

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1.4 · Connectors</div>

# Connect your apps

Connectors let Claude use the tools you already work in.

- **Google Calendar** "what is on my calendar next week?"
- **Gmail** "draft a reply to the latest email from my client"
- **Notion or Drive** "find our pricing doc and summarise it"
- **Custom** wire up your own tool with a custom connector

Claude reads your data, and with your permission, takes actions for you.

Note: Famous connectors: Google Calendar, Gmail, Notion, Google Drive, Slack, and many more, plus custom connectors built on the open MCP standard. Claude only sees what you can see. Deep setup and workflows are Module 3.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1.4 · Skills and plugins</div>

# Skills and plugins

- **Skills** reusable expertise Claude loads for a task. Built-in skills make Excel sheets, slide decks, Word docs, and PDFs. You can build your own for a workflow you repeat
- **Plugins** ready-made bundles of skills and connectors for a kind of work, like sales or finance

Note: Skills are how-to packages Claude loads when relevant, the Office file creation is a skill. Custom skills codify your own workflow. Plugins bundle skills and connectors for a role. Deep dive in Module 3.

- **Claude in Chrome** a side panel on any page: summarise, fill forms, take actions
- **Scheduled tasks** run a job on a schedule, in the cloud, even with your laptop closed
- Also in **Slack**, **Excel**, **Word**, and **PowerPoint**

<p class="try"><b>Try this</b> On a long article, open the Chrome side panel: "Summarise this page in five points I can revise from."</p>

Note: Claude in Chrome and scheduled tasks are paid and still in beta, use them on trusted sites and low-risk tasks. Deeper automation is Module 3, Cowork. Keep this a quick tour.

# Your quick decision guide

When a new task arrives:

- Something you do often? Make it a **Project**
- Need a usable tool or document? Ask for an **Artifact**
- On a web page? Use **Claude in Chrome**
- Quick and simple? Just ask

Note: A one glance cheat sheet for everything in this module. Have them run it on a real task from their week.

# Common questions

- **Is this free?** Most of it. The free plan has some limits, heavier use needs Pro
- **What about my data?** You control it in settings, and never paste secrets. More on this in Module 2
- **Is using it for my work honest?** Use it to draft and learn, then make it yours and be open about it
- **What if I lose a chat?** Your edits live under the version arrows, and Projects keep your work

Note: Answer the real questions in a beginner's head. Data safety is covered in depth in Module 2, keep it to one line here.
