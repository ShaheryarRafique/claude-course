## Introduction to AI Fluency

Note: The core idea of the whole course. We define AI fluency and reframe how people think about working with AI.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1 · Objectives</div>

# What you will be able to do

- Define AI Fluency
- Explain why it is durable skills, not just prompt tricks
- Describe the shift from using AI to thinking with it

Note: Three objectives, each mapping to a teaching slide: the definition, skills-over-tricks, and the using-to-thinking-with shift. For this lesson only.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1 · The definition</div>

# What AI Fluency means

Working with AI in ways that are:

- **Effective** it reaches the goal
- **Efficient** without wasted time or effort
- **Ethical** honest, fair, responsible
- **Safe** protecting people and data

Note: Teach: walk each word at a high level, do not lecture. Why: these four become our quality bar for the whole course. Example: a student who uses AI to understand, not just copy, is effective and ethical. Forward: people usually start small, let us widen that.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1 · A mindset shift</div>

# More than a spell checker

- Common first uses: fix text, rewrite, summarise, quick answers
- All valid, but that is the shallow end
- AI can also brainstorm, explore, problem-solve, learn, and support decisions

**Using AI as a tool  →  thinking with AI**

Note: Teach: correcting a sentence is fine, but most people never leave the shallow end. Why: they miss the real value. Example: instead of "fix this email", explore "how should I frame this difficult message?" together. Forward: if AI changes so fast, what do we actually learn?

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 1 · Skills over tricks</div>

# Skills that last, not tricks that expire

- Models, tools, and interfaces change fast
- "Ten best prompts" ages quickly
- A framework builds **durable skills** for whatever comes next

Note: Teach: the trick you learn today may be useless next quarter, the way of thinking will not. Why: it protects their time. Example: knowing how to describe what you want outlasts any single template. Forward: there is a human cost to not having this.

## Why You Need AI Fluency

Note: Make the gap concrete for the learner, then meet the three ways you work with AI.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 2A · Objectives</div>

# What you will be able to do

- Explain why access to AI is not the same as fluency
- Distinguish Automation, Augmentation, and Agency
- Identify when each mode fits

Note: Lesson objectives, tight.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 2A · The core problem</div>

# Access to AI is not fluency

**Access to AI  ≠  AI Fluency**

| The gap you feel | What fluency gives you |
| --- | --- |
| A surprising answer, and you are stuck | Steer it, refine until it is right |
| You cannot put what you want into words | Describe it clearly |
| It sounds confident, but is it correct? | Judge the output |
| Unsure what is safe to share | Use it responsibly |

Everyone has the tool. Fluency is what turns it into results.

Note: Teach: everyone has access now, few use it well, that is the whole reason this course exists. Read each row as problem then fix. Why: it shows the pain and the payoff on one screen. Example: you ask for a birthday message, get something stiff and generic, and do not know how to fix it, that is the gap, a fluent person fixes it in seconds. Forward: the four fixes in the right column, steer, describe, judge, take responsibility, are exactly the four Ds we meet next. Keep Lesson 1's quality bar in mind, effective, efficient, ethical, safe.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 2A · Three relationships</div>

# Three ways we work with AI

- **Automation** AI works for you
- **Augmentation** AI works with you
- **Agency** AI acts on your behalf

Three relationships, not a ranking.

Note: Teach: these describe who does what between you and AI. Why: choosing the right one changes your results. Example: a single project may use all three. Forward: start with automation.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 2A · Automation</div>

# Automation, AI does it for you

- You define the task and the result, AI carries it out
- Best when the outcome is clear

**Human  →  instruction  →  AI  →  result**

Examples: summarise a document, draft an email, translate, plan a trip, turn notes into action items.

Note: Teach: you are the boss handing over a well-defined job. Why: fast and reliable when you know what you want. Example: "summarise these notes into action items." Forward: but what if you do not know what you want yet? Do not present automation as primitive, it is valuable.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 2A · Augmentation</div>

# Augmentation, AI works with you

- You and AI think and create together
- AI as a thinking, creative, and problem-solving partner
- Best when the answer is not obvious

**Human  ↔  AI**

Examples: brainstorm a presentation, explore a business problem, develop a story character, weigh options.

Note: Teach: here AI does not do the work for you, it helps you do your work better. Why: this is where the biggest value often hides. Example: bouncing ideas back and forth to shape a hard decision. Forward: and when AI acts more on its own?

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 2A · Agency</div>

# Agency, AI acts on your behalf

- You set the goal, context, and boundaries, not every step
- AI then acts more independently toward it

**Human  →  goal + boundaries  →  AI  →  actions**

Script writer (automation)  →  director (agency).

Note: Teach: you move from giving exact steps to setting a vision and guardrails. Why: powerful, but it needs clear boundaries. Example: "sort my inbox by urgency and draft the routine replies." Forward: let us compare all three. Agency is not unlimited freedom.

---

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 2A · Compare the modes</div>

# None is better, each has its place

| Mode | Relationship | Works well when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automation | AI works for me | The outcome is clear |
| Augmentation | AI works with me | Exploration is needed |
| Agency | AI acts on my behalf | Independent action helps |

AI is not only a tool. It can be a tool, a medium, a partner, or a co-creator.

Note: Teach: read row by row, the point is fit, not hierarchy. Why: otherwise learners assume agency is the goal. Example: a report, automate the summary, augment the argument. Forward: different relationships need shared skills, the four Ds.

<div class="lesson-no">Lesson 2B · How they fit together</div>

# One connected habit, not four steps

**Delegation  →  Description  ↔  Discernment  →  Diligence**

| Step | The D | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Delegation | Decide what is yours, and what is AI's |
| 2 | Description | Tell AI clearly what you need |
| 3 | Discernment | Judge what comes back, and loop to sharpen it |
| 4 | Diligence | Own the final result before it goes out |

Set it up, describe and judge in a loop, then own it.

Note: Core purpose: show how the four relate, as simply as possible, not re-teach each one. Read the flow, Delegation sets up at the start, Description and Discernment loop in the middle, that double arrow is the engine, Diligence closes it out. Say the one-line summary: set it up, describe and judge in a loop, then own it. Optional live anchor: walk the birthday message from Lesson 2A through the flow. Keep it light, each D and the loop get full lessons later. Forward: upcoming lessons deepen how AI works and each competency in practice, do not imply the module is finished.
