## Lesson 1 slide — What AI Fluency means

Hook: Here is the definition we will use for the whole course.

Talking points:
- AI fluency is working with AI in ways that are effective, efficient, ethical, and safe.
- Effective means you actually reach your goal.
- Efficient means you get there without wasting time and effort.
- Ethical means you use it honestly and fairly.
- And safe means you protect people and information.

Why it matters: these four words become our quality bar. Every time you work with AI, you can ask, was that effective, efficient, ethical, and safe?

Example: a student who uses AI to genuinely understand a topic, not just copy an answer, is being both effective and ethical.

Transition: most people, though, only use a small slice of what AI can do. Let's widen that.

## Lesson 1 slide — More than a spell checker

Hook: When people first use AI, they usually use it for small clean-up jobs.

Talking points:
- Fixing text, rewriting, summarising, a quick answer. All useful, and all completely valid.
- But that is the shallow end of the pool.
- AI can also brainstorm with you, explore ideas, help you solve problems, help you learn, and support your decisions.
- The shift we are after is this: from using AI as a tool, to thinking with AI.

Why it matters: most people never leave the shallow end, and they miss the real value.

Example: instead of "fix this email", try "help me figure out how to say this difficult thing to my team". That is thinking with AI.

Transition: but AI changes so fast. So what is actually worth learning?

## Lesson 1 slide — Skills that last, not tricks that expire

Hook: Here is the trap. Most AI advice is a list of tricks.

Talking points:
- The best ten prompts. Which button to press. Which tool is hot this month.
- The problem is that all of that ages fast. Models change, tools change, the tricks change.
- A framework is different. It builds durable skills, the kind that still work no matter which AI you are using or what year it is.

Why it matters: it protects the time you invest in learning. You are not learning this week's fad.

Example: knowing how to clearly describe what you want will help you with any AI tool, forever. A specific prompt template might be useless next quarter.

Transition: so we know what fluency is, and why the skills last. Next, we make the case for why fluency is genuinely needed, and meet the three ways we work with AI. That is Lesson 2A.
