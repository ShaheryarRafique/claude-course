# Module 2 — Examples Bank (from real tutorials)

Every concrete example, demo, and prompt worth using, drawn from Anthropic's own tutorials, top YouTube videos, articles, and Udemy courses we researched. Use these to follow proven footsteps. The one we chose for the course is marked "OURS".

## Artifacts (the most example-rich feature)

Anthropic's own tutorial demos:
- Mood Canvas, an Email Writing Assistant, a Language Tutor chat interface, a Project Dashboard Generator (Gantt charts, progress bars).

From YouTube and articles:
- Tip calculator, unit converter, flashcard app, small-business inventory tracker, custom FAQ chatbot, educational games, a landing page.
- Screenshot to working tool: paste a photo of a task board and get an interactive tracker in one prompt.

OURS (the live build): an "Exam Countdown and Study Tracker" React artifact, with a checkbox and hours per subject, an animated progress bar, saved to localStorage, then Published. Exercise variant: a chemistry flashcard app with flip animation and a known counter.

Key trick every tutorial uses: say "React artifact" and "single file" to get a rendered app, not a code block.

## Projects

- Anthropic: one Project per work stream, with a knowledge base of client docs or style guides.
- Articles: a "Marketing" Project named "Q3 Product Launch, Email Sequence"; a brand-voice Project; a Project per subject or semester for students.
- Strong instruction example (paste): "Audience: senior engineering managers. Tone: formal but approachable. About 1200 words, H2 and H3 only. Never invent citations. End each section with a one sentence takeaway."

OURS: a "Final Exam Prep" Project with tutor instructions, a syllabus, a past paper, and a "my weak topics" file. The lesson is the before and after: the same prompt in a plain chat versus inside the Project.

## Memory

- Article example: instead of retyping "I am a B2B SaaS marketer, concise formal tone, audience CTOs" every session, say it once.
- OURS: "Add to memory: I am preparing for finals, target 85 percent, and I like a three line summary first."

## Files and images

- "Summarise sections 2 to 4 in five bullets, and quote one line to prove you read it."
- Compare two documents and list the differences.
- Analyse a photo of a utility bill or a spreadsheet.
- OURS: upload your syllabus, "list the ten topics most likely on the exam and quote the line that says so"; and "list every thermodynamics question in this paper with its page number."
- Send a screenshot of an error and ask what is wrong.

## Web search

- "Today's dollar to rupee rate" or today's gold rate.
- Recent news or changes in your field, with citations.
- OURS: "Search the web for this year's exam pattern for my subject", then click a citation to verify.

## Model choice, effort, and the triad

- "Explain entropy simply", run on the fast model and the deep model, compare speed and depth.
- The triad tasks: a quick fact (web search), a judgement like "which laptop fits my budget" (extended thinking), a full report (research).
- Extended thinking demos from Udemy: SWOT, a pre-mortem, a devil's advocate and risk matrix.
- OURS: "Plan a seven day study schedule for my exam" on fast versus deep.

## Conversations

- Edit an earlier message, change "formal" to "casual", to branch a new version.
- Regenerate or Retry for a better answer, optionally on another model.
- Use branching as the fix when the chat hits its maximum length.

## Voice and mobile

- "Summarise my unread emails and draft replies to the two most urgent."
- OURS: "Quiz me on today's topic" and "plan my day."

## Claude in Chrome

- Summarise the page you are on: "summarise this article in five points."
- Fill a form on a site, compare products across tabs, help with email.

## Scheduled tasks

- A daily morning brief that reads your email and calendar.
- A weekly review of your numbers.
- OURS: "Every morning, give me a five point brief on my subject and one thing to revise today."

## Prompting, before and after (for teaching quality)

- "Write an email about the project delay" becomes "Email our client that the launch slips two weeks. It is the second delay, so stay professional but apologetic."
- "Write in an Apple-like tone" becomes: give an example of the tone, then ask.

## Everyday use cases to name (breadth)

Emails, summaries of long reports, reading a contract in plain English, a meeting transcript into action items, analysing a spreadsheet, planning a trip or event, study help, research with sources.
