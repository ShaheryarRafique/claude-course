## 1. Module goals (objectives slide)

By the end of Module 1 you can: explain what Claude is, set up your account, and finish your first real task with confidence.

## 2. What is Claude, and who is Anthropic

- Made by Anthropic, a safety-first AI company. Claude is trained to be **helpful, honest, and harmless** (Constitutional AI).
- More than a chatbot: a **thinking partner** for writing, summaries, planning, research, reasoning, and coding.
- Even the **free plan is genuinely capable** (a strong default model, plus Projects, file uploads, and Memory).
- Simple analogy that works for beginners: Claude is like a very capable new employee. It is smart, but it needs clear direction from you.

## 3. See Claude in action: plan a Lahore weekend trip (the hook)

Research confirms trip planning is a top beginner "wow" use case. A good demo shows Claude produce:
- A day-by-day itinerary with realistic travel times
- Notes on when attractions open and close
- Lunch and food suggestions (local spots)
- A budget breakdown
- Ideas the user would not have thought of

This is relatable, visual, and proves the "thinking partner" idea in 60 seconds.

## 4. How to get Claude (access and setup)

- Go to **claude.ai** in a browser, click **Sign Up**. Options: Google, Apple, or email. **No credit card needed** for the free tier.
- Available on **web, desktop app, and mobile app**; everything syncs when signed in.
- What you need: any device and an internet connection. Nothing to install to start (web works immediately).

## 5. Free, Pro, or Max (options)

Current 2026 tiers (re-verify before recording):
- **Free** — a capable default model, Projects, file uploads, Memory. Limits are low, they will frustrate heavy real work.
- **Pro (about $20/month)** — roughly 5x the capacity. The right pick if Claude is a daily work tool.
- **Max (about $100 or $200/month)** — for all-day, heavy use (long coding, research, analysis).
- Also Team and Enterprise for organisations.
- **How to choose:** start Free to learn, move to Pro when limits interrupt real work, Max only if Pro is not enough.
- Briefly mention the **model picker** (a fast everyday model vs a deeper thinking model).

## 6. Your turn: first real task

Great first prompts to suggest (start tiny, one goal):
- "Summarise these notes into five bullet points."
- "Rewrite this paragraph so it is clear and professional."
- "Write a polite email to my boss asking for leave."
- "Plan a one-day trip to [their city] on a small budget."
Belmont's guide advice: pick one tiny goal you can do in two minutes, so the first win is fast.

## 7. How to work with Claude well (the four habits + beginner mistakes)

The most valuable, research-backed part. Common beginner mistakes to teach against:
- **Vague requests.** Treat Claude like a capable employee that needs clear direction. The bottleneck is usually context, not the model.
- **One giant conversation for everything.** Start a fresh chat for each new task so answers stay focused and clean.
- **Giving up after a bad answer.** Most failures are prompt failures, not capability failures. Add detail and try again.
- **Being too formal.** Type naturally, like chatting with a helpful colleague.
- The first prompt anchors the conversation, so make it the most specific one.

Map these to our four habits: Delegation (pick the task), Description (be specific, give context), Discernment (judge the answer), Diligence (verify, and be honest).

## 7b. Deep material for the four habits (with examples)

The four habits come from the AI Fluency Framework (Dakan and Feller with Anthropic). A weak link breaks the whole chain: a perfect prompt cannot save a poor delegation choice, and a great output is worthless if you never check it.

- **Delegation** decide what to hand over. Two modes: hand-off (Claude does it: format this messy data, convert this file) and partnership (you think together: brainstorm trip ideas, work through a decision).
- **Description** say what you want, clearly. Give task, context, role, format, and limits. Role example that works: "I work as a school teacher in Lahore. Give me three ways you could help me in a normal week."
- **Discernment** judge what comes back. Is it accurate? Does it sound like me? Did it miss anything?
- **Diligence** own the result. Verify facts and names, remove anything private, take responsibility for what you send.

Worked example (one task, all four habits): declining a client discount by email.
- Delegation: let Claude draft it.
- Description: "Write a four sentence reply declining the 20 percent discount, stay polite, keep the door open for future work."
- Discernment: read it, check the tone and that it promises nothing wrong.
- Diligence: fix the client name, remove internal notes, then send.

## 8b. Deep material for beginner mistakes (before and after)

Teach with real before and after pairs (Anthropic's strongest device):
- Vague to specific: "Help me with my email" becomes "Write a polite reply declining the discount, keep the door open."
- One giant chat to a fresh chat per task, so answers stay focused.
- Giving up to iterating: instead of restarting, say "make it shorter and warmer."
- Question first to document first: paste the document, then ask your question at the end (better on long inputs).
- Prohibitions to positive instructions: "do not be formal" becomes "write casually, like texting a friend."

## 8. Using Claude responsibly

- Do not paste passwords, secrets, or sensitive client data.
- Verify facts, especially numbers and names. Cite honestly. Do not plagiarise.
- Know the data and privacy settings.
